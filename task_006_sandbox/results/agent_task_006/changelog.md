# Changelog — Task 006

## Evaluation 24
**Change:** Applied pre-registered rule D1's "large history effect" branch. Phase B will not be plain GP-BO/LogEI on raw x. Before committing budget to the D1 fallback (TuRBO with Thompson sampling), I am extending the replicate-probe phase by 1–2 evaluations. Eval 25 re-evaluates x6 (the incumbent) immediately after eval 24 evaluated the same x6, to see whether the history effect persists between back-to-back calls or jumps on every call.
**Reason:** The replicate pairs differed by δ = 7.47 (centre: −2.89 at eval 1 vs −10.36 at eval 23) and 8.57 (x6: −15.71 at eval 6 vs −7.13 at eval 24). With s = 4.55 over the 22 phase-A1 points, that is δ = 1.6–1.9·s, far above the 0.5·s threshold. Spearman ρ between eval index and y is 0.18 (p = 0.40) over evals 1–24, below the 0.4 trend threshold, so the D1 rule picks TuRBO-TS over an index-augmented GP. Offline checks (no evaluations) agree that x alone currently explains almost nothing: a GP on x has leave-one-out Spearman +0.03 with fitted noise 0.28 (standardized), and a GP on x plus eval index is worse (LOO ρ = −0.42, overfit). The only sequential hint is a lag-1 autocorrelation of y of +0.37 (p = 0.08). If back-to-back repeats agree, a persistent history state exists and paired comparisons become possible. If they don't, the effect acts like large per-call noise, and the search should optimize the mean level with a noise-aware model. These two cases call for different phase-B designs, so one evaluation is worth spending to tell them apart.

It also adds a third long-gap replicate (x12, eval 12 vs eval 26). Both results feed the time-component hyperparameters of the phase-B model.

## Evaluation 28
**Change:** Phase B (from eval 29) uses a spatio-temporal GP-BO, labelled `st_gp_bo`. I chose this over both the planned plain GP-BO and D1's no-trend fallback (TuRBO with Thompson sampling). The model is y(x, t) = g(x) + s(t) + ε with an additive kernel: Matern-5/2 ARD on the 11 coordinates (dimension-scaled lengthscale prior) plus Matern-3/2 on the evaluation index t/100. The acquisition is LogEI (best_f = best observed y), optimized over x with the time input fixed at the next index (10 restarts, 1024 raw samples). Effectively this is D1's "index as a 12th GP input" branch, but with an additive rather than a product kernel. The rest of the roadmap is unchanged (D3 GP check at eval 45, D4 stagnation rule, phase C by eval 75).
**Reason:** The alternation probe shows the history effect is a persistent state that wanders slowly, not a monotone trend (which D1's trend test looks for) and not per-call noise (which TuRBO-TS on raw values assumes). At the same x within 2–3 evals, values agree to about 1: x12 read −10.47 / −9.67 at evals 26 / 28, and x6 read −7.13 / −6.38 / −7.41 at evals 24 / 25 / 27. Over long gaps they move by 3–9 units in either direction (x6 −15.71 → −7.4, x12 −13.65 → −10.1, centre −2.89 → −10.36). Reading the series as a shared offset gives a high level at evals 1–3, low at 4–13, high at 14–22 and middle at 23–28. TuRBO would centre its trust region on an incumbent (x6) whose low value came mostly from that offset. In one-step-ahead prediction over evals 15–28, the additive model had the lowest RMSE (3.30, vs 4.08 for an x-only GP and 4.26 for "repeat last y"). A product-kernel index GP had overfit earlier (LOO ρ = −0.42). The fitted model has a temporal lengthscale ≈ 2.5 evals and says only a few coordinates matter (x10 lengthscale 0.21, x9 0.35, x0 0.85; the rest > 1.1).

## Evaluation 44
**Change:** Ended phase B and started phase C (local trust-region BO, labelled `st_trbo_local`) at eval 45 instead of ~73. Phase C keeps the spatio-temporal GP and the D5 rules, with two adaptations for the drifting offset:
1. The trust-region centre is the evaluated point with the lowest GP posterior mean at the next time index, not the point with the lowest raw y.
2. "Improving" (for D5's halve / double counters) means beating the best y observed since phase C started. That running best is initialized to the centre's posterior mean at eval 45.

The box is centred there with side L·wᵢ, where wᵢ are the x-lengthscales normalized to geometric mean 1, as in TuRBO. L starts at 0.4. LogEI inside the box keeps best_f = best observed y.
**Reason:** Pre-registered rule D4 triggered at eval 40, though I only noticed it after the chunk ending at eval 44: none of the 12 BO evals 29–40 beat the best of −15.707 (eval 6) by the required 0.157 (1% of the phase-A1 range). The other checks at this point:
- D3: the ST-GP's LOO Spearman over all 44 points is 0.379 (≥ 0.3), so the GP is kept rather than switching to TuRBO.
- D6: one-step-ahead residuals vs distance to the nearest previous point give ρ = −0.09 (p = 0.72, n = 20), so there is no proximity effect.

The BO has already converged on a region (x0 ≈ 0, x9 ≈ 0, x10 ≈ 0.67), where 13 of 16 evals read between −8.9 and −14.3, against a mean of −7.0 for the phase-A1 points. The outliers (eval 36: −5.6, eval 40: −4.2) differ mainly in x1, and the model now ranks x1 (lengthscale 0.37) and x5 (0.50) as relevant next to x9 (0.26) and x10 (0.20). The raw incumbent x6 (−15.71) is a poor centre: it re-measured −7.1 to −7.4 at evals 24–27. Measured against that stale value, every local step would count as a failure and the trust region would collapse within ~15 evals for reasons unrelated to the local landscape. That is why I changed the centre and the improvement reference.

## Evaluation 52
**Change:** Paused the trust-region loop for 2 re-evaluation probes (labelled `replicate_probe`): eval 53 re-evaluates x49 (originally −13.52, gap of 4 evals) and eval 54 re-evaluates x46 (originally −14.13, gap of 8 evals). The trust region resumes, or is redesigned, based on the outcome.
**Reason:** Evals 29–52 sit in one region (x0 ≈ 0, x9 ≈ 0, x10 ≈ 0.66) with mean −10.71 and sd 2.65, and neither model can explain that spread. Within the cluster, LOO Spearman is −0.54 for an x-only GP and −0.58 for the ST GP. The refit ST model's temporal lengthscale has collapsed to 0.73 evals, which means it now treats the time component as white noise. Lag-1 autocorrelation within the cluster is −0.28 (p = 0.19). Small moves produce big changes, e.g. eval 49 → 50 moved ~0.1 in x6–x8 and y went −13.52 → −9.19. But same-x repeats earlier were consistent to about 1 within 2–3 evals (x6: −7.13, −6.38, −7.41; x12: −10.47, −9.67). Two explanations remain, and they call for different phase-C designs. If the spread is fine-scale structure in x (a rugged landscape), re-measured points keep their values, and small-step local search around the best points pays off. If the spread is history noise, re-measured points fall back toward the cluster mean of ≈ −10.7, and the remaining budget is best spent sampling the region's best predicted mean. Re-measuring the two best recent points decides this, and each re-measurement could also set a record if the value holds.

## Evaluation 54
**Change:** Dropped the trust-region mechanics of phase C (D5 halve/double/restart counters). From eval 55 to the end of the budget, every point is chosen by global spatio-temporal GP LogEI (the same model and acquisition as phase B), labelled `st_gp_bo_endgame`. The acquisition is optimized over the full box with the time input fixed at the next index, and best_f = best observed y.
**Reason:** The re-evaluation probes show that the spread within the region is history nuisance, not fine-scale structure in x. x49 went −13.52 (eval 49) → −8.25 (eval 53), and x46 went −14.13 (eval 46) → −9.13 (eval 54). Both fell back to the cluster mean of −10.71 (sd 2.65 over evals 29–52). A trust region whose success/failure counters react to that nuisance is not tracking the local landscape. The centre moved to a different "lucky" point on 7 of the 8 phase-C steps (#41, #42, #46, #47, #42, #49, #50, #50), and evals 47–52 counted as 6 straight failures (L halved twice, 0.4 → 0.1) with no evidence the mean had changed. Refit on all 54 points, the ST GP's temporal lengthscale is 0.74 evals, so the time component acts as observation noise. Its posterior-mean minimum over the box is −11.26 (sd 2.79 at the next index), at x ≈ (0, 0.13, 0.53, 0.06, 0.23, 0.03, 0.29, 0.40, 0.48, 0, 0.68), inside the region already being sampled. No other region is predicted lower. Under this model, LogEI at the next index is expected improvement of the next observed value over the record (−15.707). That is the quantity that matters for the final result. It balances sampling the best-mean region (each draw has about a 5% chance of beating the record) against exploring regions where the GP is still uncertain about g. Step sizes back this up: consecutive moves under 0.3 change y by a median of 1.98, against 3.00 for moves over 0.8.

## Evaluation 64
**Change:** For evals 65–70, LogEI (same ST-GP model, next time index) is maximized only over "fresh" candidates at least 0.35 (Euclidean) from every previously evaluated point. The candidate set is 30k uniform points plus 30k points that keep the model-relevant coordinates (x0, x1, x9, x10) near the posterior-mean argmin (Gaussian jitter, sd 0.05) and draw x2–x8 uniformly. Label `st_gp_bo_fresh`. After eval 70 I compare these values with the recent same-region values to decide the rest of the budget.
**Reason:** Evals 55–64 under unconstrained LogEI stayed in the same region and read mean −9.58 (sd 1.4), with no record. Within that region (34 points, evals 30–64), y worsens with eval index (Spearman ρ = +0.40, p = 0.018). The 12-eval block means were −10.45 (evals 29–40), −11.16 (41–52) and −9.43 (53–64). y also rises with local sampling density (ρ = +0.26 / +0.26 / +0.33 for kernel widths 0.1 / 0.2 / 0.4, p = 0.05–0.14). The D6 statistic at eval 64 is ρ = −0.21 (p = 0.19, n = 40) for one-step residual vs distance to the nearest previous point. That is in the "close to history comes out worse" direction but under the 0.4 threshold, so D6 does not formally fire. One-step residuals average +0.87, i.e. points come out worse than the model expects. Four of five revisits got worse (x6, x12, x49, x46), which selection bias alone could also explain. If the history effect depletes regions that are sampled repeatedly, fresh points with the same model-relevant coordinates should read lower than the region's recent values. If it is a shared drift, they should read about the same. The weakly relevant coordinates x2–x8 (lengthscales 0.57–1.59) give room to move ≥ 0.35 away from all history at little cost in predicted g, so the test costs little under either explanation.

## Evaluation 70

No trust region and no minimum-distance constraint. This exploits the lowest-g region and relies on the model's time component to pick up a low phase of the shared offset.
**Reason:** The fresh-point test did not support depletion. The six fresh points (min distance 0.39–0.69 to history) read −11.30, −4.16, −9.35, −6.90, −6.16, −7.88: mean −7.62, worse than both the region's recent mean (−9.43 for evals 53–64) and the model's own predictions (−8.9 to −10.4). A decomposition of the ST-GP posterior on all 70 points explains the recent drift as a global offset rather than local depletion. In the best region the estimated g sits at about −12 (5-eval blocks: −11.99, −11.52, −11.92, −12.12, −12.28 over evals 41–65). The estimated offset s(t) climbed steadily over the same period: +0.05, +0.94, +2.11, +2.83, +2.13, then +3.23 for evals 66–70, so the fresh points carried the same high offset. The design phase showed swings of similar size with spread-out points (s = −2.42 at evals 6–10, +2.76 at 16–20). Residuals y − g are autocorrelated (lag 1/2/3/5/8: 0.50/0.42/0.30/0.12/0.01), so the offset persists for about 3–5 evals. The model estimates moving far in x2–x8 costs about +1.6 in g (−10.65 for the fresh block). The record (−15.707 at eval 6) is a residual of about −8 on g ≈ −7.8, so beating it needs the lowest-g region plus a low swing of the offset. LogEI at the next index is the acquisition that weights both.

## Evaluation 90
**Change:** For the final 10 evaluations (91–100), switched the acquisition from LogEI on the latent function to probability of improvement on the next observed value (posterior including the fitted observation noise), labelled `st_gp_pi_final`. Same spatio-temporal GP, refit every step. The acquisition is maximized over 40k uniform candidates plus 40k Gaussian perturbations (sd 0.03–0.10) of the 5 evaluated points with the lowest posterior mean, then polished with L-BFGS-B. Each next point maximizes P(y_next < best observed y).
**Reason:** With 10 evaluations left, information from exploring can no longer be put to use, so the only thing that matters is the chance that each remaining observation sets a record (best = −15.707, eval 6). The refit model now assigns a substantial share of the variance to observation noise (noise 0.25–0.28 in standardized units since eval 80, up from 0.007), with temporal lengthscale ≈ 2.6 evals. Latent-function LogEI ignores that noise and over-rewards epistemic variance. Its picks during evals 81–90 had predicted means of only −8.5 to −10.7 and realized −3.08 / −3.35 at evals 83–84 (predicted −8.90 / −9.17). Points in the main region (x0 ≈ 0, x1 ≈ 0.12, x9 ≈ 0.04, x10 ≈ 0.6–0.7) are predicted lowest, e.g. eval 87 was predicted −12.35 and came back −11.15. Maximizing P(y_obs < record) puts more weight on a low mean than LogEI does, which is the right priority for the final evaluations.
