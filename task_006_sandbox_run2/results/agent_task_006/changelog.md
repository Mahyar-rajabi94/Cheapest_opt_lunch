# Task 006 — Changelog (append-only)

## Evaluation 22
**Change:** Deviation criterion X5 triggered. When Phase D (local trust region) starts, its trust region will be centred on the evaluated point with the lowest GP posterior mean, not on the raw best observed value.
**Reason:** The repeat probes showed a history effect larger than the whole spread of the data. Re-evaluating the best LHS point (#15, x unchanged) moved y from −11.517 to −0.941 (Δ = +10.58). Re-evaluating the median LHS point (#17) moved y from −6.680 to −8.900 (Δ = −2.22). With s = q90 − q10 of the 20 LHS values = 10.165, r = max|Δ|/s = 1.04, above X5's threshold of 0.5. So a single low observation says little about what the same location returns later. (Pre-registered branches: D1 → r > 0.02, so the history-aware branch applies; D2 → LHS skewness 0.28 < 1.0, so no Yeo–Johnson warping.)

## Evaluation 22
**Change:** Deviated from rule D1's history-aware branch. D1 called for a GP on (x, t), a product kernel over x and the evaluation index, with qLogNoisyEI at t = next index (label `gp_bo_tnei`). Phase C instead uses a GP on x only, with learned (large) noise variance, and qLogNoisyEI with all evaluated x as the baseline (label `gp_bo_nei_x`). Phase D will use the same model. Budget split and all other rules are unchanged.
**Reason:** After evaluation 22 the (x, t) GP fitted a time lengthscale of 0.044, i.e. about 4 evaluations. With a product kernel, this makes the posterior at the next t ignore almost all of the data (in effect a ~5-point sliding window in 11-D), so it cannot represent persistent x-structure. The data do show persistent x-structure. Across the 20 LHS points, evaluated at evals 1–20, Spearman(x1, y) = +0.67 (p ≈ 0.001) and Spearman(x2, y) = −0.39. The x-only GP picks up the same structure: shortest ARD lengthscales on x1 (0.48), x2 (0.51) and x11 (0.71), and a learned noise sd of 0.57 (standardized units) that absorbs the history effect. The two models' one-step-ahead forecasts over evals 13–22 do not favour the time input: Spearman(pred, obs) = −0.07 (x-only) vs −0.01 (x, t), RMSE 4.72 vs 4.68. So there is no evidence that the t input helps, and clear evidence that it discards the persistent signal. Noisy EI is used instead of plain LogEI because the observed best (−11.517) is now known not to be a reliable incumbent level.

## Evaluation 69
**Change:** Deviation criterion X1 triggered. Phase C (global GP-BO, `gp_bo_nei_x`) ends at eval 69 instead of 76. Phase D (local trust-region BO, `trbo_local`) starts at eval 70 and gets all 31 remaining evaluations instead of 24. As pre-committed under X5 (entry at eval 22), the trust region is centred on #61, the evaluated point with the lowest GP posterior mean (−11.35; observed −10.41). It is not centred on the observed best, #54 (observed −14.43, posterior mean only −9.96). L0 = 0.4 and all other Phase D settings are as in the roadmap.
**Reason:** The last new best was eval 54 (y = −14.432). Evals 55–69 (15 consecutive Phase-C evaluations) did not improve on it. Phase C was still sampling good values (median of evals 23–69 = −10.21, 53% below −10, against an LHS median of −6.93). But its proposals had settled on one basin: x1 = 0, x2 ≈ 0.3–0.6, x3 = x4 = x9 = 1, x5 = x7 = x11 = 0. The global acquisition was no longer finding better values there, which is the situation X1 was written for. In the model's view, the observed best is mostly a favourable history draw: its posterior mean is 4.5 units above its observed value, consistent with the +10.6 repeat-probe shift. So the X5 centre is used.

## Evaluation 78
**Change:** Switched Phase D's trust-region bookkeeping to a noise-aware variant, label `trbo_pm_local`, for evals 79–100. Same x-only GP and qLogNoisyEI acquisition as before. (a) The centre is re-chosen before every proposal as the evaluated point with the lowest GP posterior mean (X5's rule, now applied continuously rather than once). (b) A step counts as a success only if the new point's posterior mean, after refitting, beats the previous incumbent's posterior mean by > 1e-3·s. Comparing against the raw best observed value is dropped. (c) The side length restarts at L = 0.4, keeps the 3-success-double / 2-failure-halve schedule, and has a floor of L = 0.2. This replaces the collapse threshold of 0.01, so X3's collapse-restart can no longer fire, but X6 stays in force (halving respects the floor; a second X6 trigger still restarts per X3). (d) The duplicate guard is widened from L2 distance 1e-3 to 0.1: candidates within 0.1 of any evaluated point are rejected.
**Reason:** The plain TR (evals 70–78) found a new best at eval 72 (y = −15.707) but then counted all six follow-ups as failures and shrank from L = 0.4 to 0.05. Its reference value was a noise outlier: the GP's posterior mean at #72 is −10.04, 5.7 units above the observation, i.e. about 2.5 noise sd (learned noise sd ≈ 2.24 in y units). #54 is the same: observed −14.43, posterior mean −9.90. Measured against such a draw, nearly every honest local step "fails". The TR would have collapsed to sub-noise step sizes in ~4–6 more evals, which cannot be resolved when per-evaluation history noise is ~2.2 units. For (d): the exact repeat at eval 21 shifted by +10.6. Across evals 23–78, points whose nearest earlier evaluation is closer than 0.7 averaged residuals (observed − predicted) of +1.7 to +1.8, against +0.6 for points farther than 0.7. That proximity trend is not significant (Spearman p = 0.16), but it points the same way as the repeat, so tiny steps next to existing points are not worth the risk.

## Evaluation 86
**Change:** For the final 14 evaluations (87–100), replaced the noise-aware trust region (`trbo_pm_local`) with a proximity-aware GP-BO step, label `gp_dnn_eiobs`. (a) The GP gets a 12th input: the L2 distance from each point to the nearest point evaluated before it (scaled by 1/1.5, clipped to [0, 1]). For a candidate, this is the distance to the nearest of all points evaluated so far. (b) The acquisition is the log expected improvement of the new *observation* (posterior predictive including the learned noise) over the actual record y = −15.707. This replaces latent-f qLogNoisyEI. (c) It is optimized over the full box (20 restarts, 2048 raw samples). The 0.1 minimum-separation guard stays. X6 stays in force: if 6 consecutive proposals each come in worse than predicted by > 0.1·s, I revert to `trbo_pm_local`.
**Reason:** Over evals 23–86 (64 model-driven points), residuals (observed − predicted) worsen steadily as the nearest earlier evaluation gets closer: mean +1.92 for distance < 0.3 (n = 11), +1.82 for 0.3–0.6 (n = 12), +0.88 for 0.6–0.9 (n = 11), +0.58 for ≥ 0.9 (n = 30). All four values below −14 came from points ≥ 0.6 from any earlier evaluation. The best value within 0.6 of an earlier point was −12.77. Adding the distance feature to the GP raises the fitted marginal log-likelihood from −1.718 to −1.672 per point (about +4.0 nats over 86 points, a likelihood-ratio statistic of ~8 on one extra parameter). It lowers the learned noise sd from 0.70 to 0.58, and the feature gets a relevant lengthscale of 0.62, so part of what was treated as unpredictable history noise is explained by proximity. This, plus the +10.6 shift on the exact repeat at eval 21, means local steps around existing points are systematically penalized. That is exactly what the trust region does. Evals 79–86 under `trbo_pm_local` produced no new best (best of them −11.90). The new model's posterior means put the basin at ≈ −11 against a record of −15.71, so the realistic route to a lower returned value is a fresh, distant point in the good basin with a favourable draw. EI computed on the observation, rather than the latent mean, scores exactly that: with noise sd ≈ 2 y-units, latent EI over a −15.7 record is ~0 everywhere. The dry proposal before this switch shows the trade-off. A fresh point in the basin (distance 0.97 from any earlier point) scores log-EI −3.81, against −7.12 for re-sampling the current posterior-mean centre.

## Evaluation 93
**Change:** Removed the nearest-previous-distance input from the GP for the final 7 evaluations (94–100), label `gp_bo_eiobs`. The GP goes back to x only (11 inputs, learned noise), as in Phases C/D. Everything else from the eval-86 entry is kept: log-EI of the new observation (posterior predictive including noise) over the record −15.707, full-box optimization with 20 restarts and 2048 raw samples, and the 0.1 minimum-separation guard.
**Reason:** The proximity hypothesis behind the eval-86 change failed its out-of-sample check. The 7 points chosen under it (evals 87–93, all fresh: distance 0.78–1.34 from any earlier point) came in worse than predicted by +3.4 on average (residuals +0.9, +4.5, −0.4, +7.6, +4.9, +2.0, +4.4). The best of them was −12.05. With them included, Spearman(residual, nearest-previous distance) over evals 23–93 weakened from −0.17 to −0.09 (p = 0.47). Points ≥ 0.9 from any earlier evaluation now average y = −9.62, against −8.98 for those closer than 0.3, a gap of 0.6 units, inside the noise. The feature's marginal-likelihood gain dropped from +4.0 nats to +0.72 nats over 93 points. That no longer justifies the extra input, and the feature was making the acquisition over-value distant, poorly modelled regions. Observation-level EI over the record is kept because the target is the lowest *returned* value and the learned noise sd (~0.74 standardized, ≈ 2.5 y-units) is large relative to the gap between the basin's posterior mean (≈ −11) and the record.

## Evaluation 100
**Change:** No new decision; this is an after-the-fact record of what evals 95–100 actually did. They are labelled `gp_bo_eiobs`, but they did not follow that method as intended. #95 and #97 were the best of the few poor, distant optimizer restarts that survived the 0.1 separation guard. #96 and #98–#100 (shown as `logEIobs=nan` in my run log) were uniformly random points from the routine's last-resort fallback. The campaign ended at the budget with best y = −15.707 (eval 72), unchanged since eval 72.
**Reason:** This was a design error in the eval-93 change, found by replaying the proposal for eval 96 offline (no evaluations used). Without the distance input, the observation-level EI over the record peaks at, or within 0.03 of, the already-evaluated points with the lowest posterior mean: 17 of 20 L-BFGS restarts converged there (log-EI −4.76). The 0.1 minimum-separation guard rejected all of those. The only survivors were distant restarts with much lower acquisition (log-EI −6.3 to −7.9, predicted y −3 to −8). When none survived, the code used a random point. In the eval-86 variant, the distance input had pushed the acquisition maximum away from existing points by itself, so this interaction never came up. My pre-run check at eval 93 only confirmed that the acquisition evaluated; it did not exercise the full proposal path with the guard. A correct fix would have optimized the acquisition with the separation constraint built in, or restricted it to the trust-region box (`trbo_pm_local`). The outcome: 6 of the 100 evaluations (6%) went to near-random points (y = −2.46, −0.05, −1.01, −6.15, −4.11, −0.04), not to fresh points in the good basin. That basin's posterior mean is ≈ −11 and per-evaluation history noise ≈ 2.5, so those 6 evaluations had only a modest chance of beating −15.707 even if spent as intended.
