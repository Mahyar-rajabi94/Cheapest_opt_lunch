## Target and features

- Target: `calculated PCE (%)` = Voc x Jsc x FF, renamed `PCE`. The reported `PCE (%)` is not used. `FF (%)` is stored as a fraction (0.24-0.84) and was not divided by 100.
- 11 numeric features, no one-hot encoding and no snapping: `HOMO_D`, `LUMO_D`, `Eg_D`, `HOMO_A`, `LUMO_A`, `Eg_A`, `DA_ratio`, `additive_conc`, `anneal_temp`, `HTL_energy`, `ETL_energy`. `HTL_energy` (3 values) and `ETL_energy` (14 values) are plain numbers.
- `Ehl_D (eV)` and `Ehl_A (eV)` are excluded: they equal LUMO - HOMO exactly (see sanity checks).
- Device outcomes and post-fabrication measurements (Voc, Jsc, FF, PCE, mobilities, thicknesses) are not features. `PCE` and `PCE_std` are only in `pool_labels.csv`.

As decided with the user for task_005 (the same 526 devices and the same checks), boundary candidates count as passing, as >= and <= intend, with a 1e-09 tolerance. ### Known flags (verify against the source papers)

Values were not changed.

- **PBDB-TF:Y6 (15.71%, the best candidate, candidate 221)** fails `gap_consistency_A`. Its acceptor optical gap is recorded as 1.81 eV against a HOMO-LUMO gap of 1.55 eV (HOMO -5.65, LUMO -4.10); Y6 is usually reported at about 1.3-1.4 eV. Its PCE is a real measurement, so it stays in the pool, but its features are likely wrong and may mislead surrogate models near the top of the pool. DOI: 10.1016/j.joule.2019.01.004
- **PBDTTz-N** fails `gap_consistency_D` in 1 candidate(s). HOMO -5.23 eV and LUMO -3.52 eV give a HOMO-LUMO gap of 1.71 eV, but its optical gap is recorded as 1.95 eV. DOI(s): 10.1039/C7TC04750A
- ETL **PDINO** is recorded at -3.90 eV in 101 rows, -2.18 eV in 1 row (of the 541 complete rows).
  The single PDINO row at -2.18 eV is an encoding inconsistency (PDINO is otherwise -3.90 eV).

## Downstream notes

- **PCE is never scaled** in any file. Each optimizer's surrogate must standardize PCE using only the candidates it has queried, refitting at every iteration.
- `query(candidate_id, x_proposed=None, log_path=...)` returns PCE from `pool_labels.csv` and appends `candidate_id`, `PCE`, `timestamp`, `repeat`, `n_experiments` and `x_proposed` to the query log. A repeat returns the stored value and does not count as a new experiment. Use a fresh log per campaign.
- `nearest_unqueried(x_unit, queried_ids)` maps a continuous proposal to the closest unqueried candidate (Euclidean distance on the `_unit` features; ties to the lowest `candidate_id`; points outside [0, 1] allowed).
- `top_k_ids(fraction=0.05)` returns the candidates at or above the (1 - fraction) PCE quantile. **For evaluation only**; an optimizer must never call it.
- All 11 features count equally in the distance, so differences in processing variables matter as much as differences in energy levels.
- Many continuous proposals will map to the same nearby candidates. The query log records both the proposed point (`x_proposed`) and the candidate it was mapped to.

## Known limitations

- **The pool is published, mostly best-case devices.** Low-PCE and failed devices are under-represented. Success in this benchmark shows efficient search of a known space, not discovery of new materials.
- **Optimizers can't go beyond the pool.** New donor-acceptor pairs or new processing conditions cannot be proposed.
- **The top of the pool is sparse.** Only 10 candidates exceed 13.4% PCE, so the last improvements are the hardest to find.
- **Same-pair candidates are correlated.** The 33 pairs with more than one candidate share the donor's energy features (29 of them all six), which can inflate apparent performance. Report distinct pairs found.
- **The best candidate's features are likely wrong.** Y6's recorded optical gap is likely incorrect, which may mislead surrogate models near the top of the pool.
- **Mapping continuous proposals is crude.** Nearest-candidate mapping weights all features equally and can send many proposals to the same few candidates.
- **No structural information.** Materials are distinguished only by their energy levels.
- **Solvent additive identity and annealing time are not features.**
- **Energy levels are noisy.** They come from mixed measurement methods and were averaged per material.
