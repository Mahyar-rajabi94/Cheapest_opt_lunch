## Target and features

- Target: `calculated PCE (%)` = Voc x Jsc x FF, renamed `PCE`. The reported `PCE (%)` is not used. `FF (%)` is stored as a fraction (0.24-0.84) and was not divided by 100.
- 11 inputs (all continuous, no one-hot encoding): `HOMO_D`, `LUMO_D`, `Eg_D`, `HOMO_A`, `LUMO_A`, `Eg_A`, `DA_ratio`, `additive_conc`, `anneal_temp`, `HTL_energy`, `ETL_energy`.
- `Ehl_D (eV)` and `Ehl_A (eV)` are excluded: they equal LUMO - HOMO exactly (see sanity checks).
- Metadata, never emulator inputs: `Donor`, `Acceptor`, `DOI`, `Donor SMILES`, `Acceptor SMILES`, `HTL`, `ETL`, `interlayer_support`, `n_replicates`, `PCE_std`.

## Downstream notes

- Every optimizer proposal goes through `prepare_query(x_unit)`: `from_unit` to original units, snap HTL_energy and ETL_energy, evaluate the constraints, and return the snapped 11-value input with a feasibility flag.
- The benchmark log should record both the **proposed** and the **snapped** HTL/ETL values (`x_proposed` and `x` in the `prepare_query` result).
- Optimizers see a staircase along HTL_energy and ETL_energy: PCE changes only at capture-interval boundaries.
- Scaled inputs are not clipped; training rows outside the bounds map outside [0, 1] and are kept.
- The target is not scaled in the saved file. The emulator may standardize PCE with all data; each optimizer's surrogate must standardize using only the points it has queried.

## Known limitations

- **Snapping gives levels unequal shares of the search space.** A level's share depends on the gaps to its neighbours, not on how many devices support it. V2O5 (2 devices) captures 41.7% of the HTL range, so a uniform random search would query it about 42% of the time. Several ETL levels with 1-2 devices also capture sizeable intervals.
- **Snapping creates a staircase.** Along HTL and ETL, PCE changes only at interval boundaries. Gradient-based optimizers may struggle along these dimensions.
- **HTL and ETL are independent.** 24 of the 42 combinations never occur in the data, and most of these mix conventional and inverted device designs. Emulator predictions there are not backed by data.
- **Energy levels vary independently.** Donor and acceptor energies are continuous, so they can form combinations that no real molecule has. The constraints limit this but do not prevent it.
- **No structural information.** Molecular structure is not an emulator input, so the emulator distinguishes materials only by their energy levels.
- **Interlayer energies mix different quantities.** HTL/ETL values combine work functions, band edges, and LUMOs, and different materials share the same value.
- **Solvent additive identity is not a feature.** 0.5% DIO and 0.5% CN look identical.
- **Annealing time is not a feature.** Temperature comes with no duration.
- **Energy levels are noisy.** They come from mixed measurement methods and were averaged per material.
- **The emulator can only interpolate.** Results near the bounds, or in sparsely sampled regions, are less reliable.
